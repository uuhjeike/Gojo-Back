In the universe of Orr, night did not follow day.

Night followed memory.

Every evening, when the last person in the world forgot something, the city of Elyra appeared.

Nobody knew where it came from.

At sunset there was nothing but a flat silver plain.

Then, somewhere beyond the horizon, a single window would glow.

Another would appear.

Then a door.

Then a staircase.

Then an entire street.

Within an hour, a city larger than any human civilization stood beneath the violet sky.

By morning, it was gone.

Everyone in Orr knew this.

Nobody questioned it.

Except a boy named Cael.

He had spent seventeen years trying to remember the city after sunrise.

He couldn't.

Every morning he woke with only one strange feeling:

Something had been there.

Something enormous.

Something beautiful.

Something that knew his name.

---

One night, Cael stayed awake.

He sat beside his bedroom window and waited.

At 11:42, he heard a bell.

The first building appeared.

At 11:43, another.

At 11:44, the street outside his house became a road made of white stone.

At 11:45, someone knocked on his door.

Cael opened it.

A woman stood outside.

She wore a black coat covered in tiny moving stars.

“You're awake,” she said.

“Yes.”

“Then we have very little time.”

“Who are you?”

“My name is Mira.”

“Why is this city here?”

She looked behind him.

“Because someone forgot the moon.”

Cael frowned.

“The moon is right there.”

He pointed upward.

The moon was enormous and green.

Mira shook her head.

“That isn't the moon.”

Cael looked again.

The green object had no light of its own.

It was watching them.

Then it blinked.

Cael stepped backward.

“What is that?”

“The moon you remember.”

---

Mira took him through the city.

Elyra was unlike any city Cael had ever imagined.

There were houses with oceans inside them.

There were clocks that measured emotions instead of time.

There were restaurants where people ate yesterday.

There were libraries containing books that had not been written yet.

In one enormous square, hundreds of people stood silently around a fountain.

The fountain flowed upward.

“What are they doing?” Cael asked.

“Waiting.”

“For what?”

“Their names.”

Cael didn't understand.

Mira pointed toward the fountain.

A man stepped forward.

The water touched his hand.

A word appeared above him.

Jon.

Everyone cheered.

The man began crying.

“What happens if their name doesn't appear?”

Mira's expression changed.

“They disappear from every memory.”

Cael looked around.

“How many people live here?”

“Tonight?”

She counted silently.

“Approximately eight billion.”

Cael stared at her.

“That's more than my entire world.”

“I know.”

---

They reached the center of Elyra.

There stood a tower.

It had no entrance.

No windows.

No roof.

Only a small golden hole in its side.

Mira gave Cael a key.

“Put it inside.”

“What happens?”

“The city remembers tomorrow.”

Cael inserted the key.

The universe stopped.

Not time.

Everything.

The stars stopped shining.

The wind stopped.

The people stopped breathing.

Even Cael's own thoughts became silent.

Then something spoke.

Not from the tower.

Not from the sky.

From inside his own memory.

You were never supposed to wake up.

Cael whispered:

“Who are you?”

The first person who dreamed you.

The tower opened.

Inside was a room.

In the middle of it sat a sleeping child.

The child was perhaps six years old.

Cael walked closer.

The child's face looked familiar.

Very familiar.

It was his own face.

Mira whispered:

“Don't wake him.”

Cael stared.

“Who is he?”

“Cael.”

“No.”

“The first Cael.”

He looked at the sleeping child.

“What happens if he wakes?”

Mira looked toward the sky.

The green moon was beginning to crack.

“If he wakes, your universe ends.”

Cael stepped away.

“Why?”

“Because this entire universe is his dream.”

---

Cael stood there for a long time.

Then he noticed something.

The sleeping child's hand was clenched.

Inside it was something tiny.

A little blue planet.

Cael slowly reached for it.

The moment he touched the planet, he saw another universe.

Then another.

Then another.

Infinite worlds.

Infinite versions of himself.

Infinite cities.

Infinite moons.

And at the very end, he saw something impossible.

A tiny bedroom.

A person sitting in front of a screen.

Reading his story.

Cael stared directly at them.

For one brief moment, their eyes met.

Then Cael smiled.

He finally understood.

The city did not appear because people forgot something.

It appeared because someone, somewhere, remembered it.

Someone was imagining it.

Someone was reading it.

Someone was keeping it alive.

Cael looked at Mira.

“What happens tomorrow?”

She smiled.

“Tomorrow is whatever they remember.”

The green moon closed its eye.

The tower disappeared.

Elyra began fading.

One building at a time.

One street at a time.

One star at a time.

Cael remained standing until the very end.

Just before everything vanished, he heard Mira's voice:

“Remember us.”

Morning arrived.

Cael woke in his ordinary bedroom.

The city was gone.

The moon was normal.

The world was exactly as it had always been.

He sat quietly for several minutes.

Then he noticed something on his desk.

A tiny blue planet.

He picked it up.

It was warm.

And somewhere, impossibly far away, a city called Elyra appeared again.

Not tomorrow.

Not tonight.

But now.

Because someone had remembered it.

And that was enough.
